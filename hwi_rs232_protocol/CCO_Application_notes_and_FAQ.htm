Troubleshooting

CCO Relays

The relays on the contact closure output board (HWI-CCO-8) are latching relays. Therefore, when power to the board is lost, the relay will remain latched in whatever state it was last in. This can sometimes give the appearance that the relay is "backwards", i.e. the contacts are still in the "ON" state when the relay coil is not energized. As long as the CCO output has been programmed into the system, it will operate properly. Outputs that have not been programmed in the system could potentially be in either state.

The rightmost DIP switch (switch 6) is the mode switch. To set the HWI-CCO-8 for test mode, put switch 6 in the ON or up position. While in test mode the pushbutton switches will toggle the state of the relays with each button press. The LEDs in the buttons indicate the position of the relays. When the LED is off, the common is connected to the normally closed terminal. When the LED is on, the common is connected to the normally open terminal. After testing is complete, the mode switch (switch 6) MUST be returned to the OFF or down position.

If the LINK LED is flashing, the board is not communicating to the processor. Also, it may not be in the database. This LED will also flash during an upload just like the keypads do.

CCI Relays

The CCI should measure 14-15V between common and the input when open. If jumpered it should read zero volts. Applying a short from the input terminal to the common terminal can test any individual input. While shorted, the associated LED should turn ON. When the short is removed, the LED should turn OFF. If the equipment connected to the input is already forcing the "ON" state, the "OFF" state can be verified by simply disconnecting the removable terminal block from the printed circuit board.

If the LINK LED is flashing, the board is not communicating to the processor. Also, it may not be in the database. This LED will also flash during an upload just like the keypads do.

Advanced Procedures

Getting a shorter pulse time than 0.52s

  1. Got to User Preferences
  2. Under Load Schedule, select CCO Pulse
  3. Here you will have the option to have a shorter pulse time. This only works with CCO boards with a date code of "M01" and newer

FAQ

  • Q: what "voltage" does a CCO create?
    A: none, just dry contact, we receive voltage on one contact, send voltage back to the device "closes loop"
  • Q: How do you take an input from a solid-state relay?
    A: If relay is solid state, you will probably need to place a relay in between the CCI and the solid-state relay.
  • Q: What is the address of the on board CCI on an 8 series processor?
    A: KBP, [01], 1 sends kbp for on board cci on proc 1 from terminal
  • Q: Is there thermal protection on the relay outputs?
    A: No, it's just a coil/mechanical relay, it has no thermal protection.
  • Q: What is the maximum distance that I can run a wire from my CCI to the contactor that triggers it?
    A: That depends on the wire type/gauge used.  There cannot be more than 200 ohms of resistance between the contactors for reliable operation.
  • Q: Will the processor check the CCI status after and upload or a power cycle?
    A:     Startup Sequencer is the only time that the processor will check the current state of a CCI on powerup.
  • Q: can I send a button press to a cco output?
    A: No use a phantom kp to do the cco open, close
